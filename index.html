Introduction

The skeletal system is the framework of 206 bones that supports movement, protects organs, produces blood cells, and stores essential minerals and fats. It is divided into the axial skeleton (skull, spine, rib cage) and the appendicular skeleton (limbs, shoulders, hips).

Functions of the Skeletal System

The skeletal system provides support, protection, movement, mineral storage, and blood cell production. Bones act as levers for muscles, shield vital organs, and serve as reservoirs for calcium and phosphorus.

Types of Bones

There are five main types of bones:
- Long Bones (femur, humerus)
- Short Bones (carpals, tarsals)
- Flat Bones (skull, sternum)
- Irregular Bones (vertebrae, sacrum)
- Sesamoid Bones (patella)

Bone Diseases

- Osteoporosis: reduced bone density, fragile bones.
- Osteopenia: lower density than normal, precursor to osteoporosis.
- Osteonecrosis: blood supply loss, bone tissue death.
- Scoliosis: abnormal curvature of the spine.
- Arthritis: inflammation of joints affecting bone movement.

Fun Facts

- The hyoid bone is the only bone not connected to others.
- Small bones heal in 6–8 weeks.
- About 1% of humans have 13 pairs of ribs.
- In zero gravity, the spine elongates and people grow taller.
- Bones are stronger than steel by weight!
